# CM Punjab Rozgar Scheme: Loan Amounts & How to Apply

Search "cm punjab rozgar scheme" and you'll find a dozen sites quoting a dozen different loan ceilings. That's not you misreading something — it's a genuine naming mess this guide untangles before it gets to eligibility, documents, and the application steps.

## What Is the CM Punjab Rozgar Scheme?

"CM Punjab Rozgar Scheme" isn't one single, officially branded program name — it's the phrase people search for a **government-backed loan program** run through **PSIC** (Punjab Small Industries Corporation) and the **Bank of Punjab** to help residents move away from **high-interest bank loans** toward affordable, **subsidized interest rates** for starting or growing a business. In practice the phrase gets used for two related things: the original **Punjab Rozgar Program**, which PSIC has run since 2020, and the newer **Asaan Karobar Finance Scheme** launched under **Chief Minister Maryam Nawaz Sharif**, disbursed through **Bank of Punjab**. Content across the web often blends the two under one search-friendly title.

The underlying goal in both cases is the same: reduce **unemployment** and support **self-employment**, **entrepreneurship**, and **job creation** for **small businesses**, **medium businesses**, and **startups**, without the collateral and paperwork that come with a **high-interest bank loan**. The scheme is meant to give people who have a workable business idea but no capital a path to **business ownership** with **dignity**, rather than staying dependent on **unstable employment**.

## Who Is Eligible for CM Punjab Rozgar Scheme?

Across every source reviewed for this guide, four eligibility points repeat consistently: you need a **valid CNIC** (a **NADRA-issued CNIC**), you must be a **permanent resident of Punjab**, you need to fall inside a stated age band, and you need either a **business idea** or an **existing business** you can describe. The age band itself varies by source — some cite **18 to 50 years**, others **20 to 50 years**, with a note that professionals may qualify up to **57**. Treat the exact cutoff as something to confirm at application time rather than a fixed number, since it may differ by loan category.

Beyond age and residency, applicants are generally expected to have a **clean credit history** with **no bank defaults**, and to show a realistic **ability to repay loan** amounts based on the business plan submitted. **Formal education is not mandatory** for most tiers — **basic schooling** or **vocational credentials** are described as helpful rather than required. **Students**, **freelancers**, and existing **business owners** are all described as eligible groups, provided they can present a **workable business concept**.

## Priority Groups Under the Scheme

Several groups are consistently named as receiving priority consideration: **youth** (commonly described as ages **18-35**), **women entrepreneurs**, **differently abled persons**, and **transgender individuals**. **First-time young entrepreneurs**, **rural skilled workers**, and residents of **Southern Punjab** are also flagged for priority in some sources, alongside **home-based business operators** who may not have a formal storefront.

The stated intent behind this prioritization is **financial inclusion** — reaching people who are typically underserved by conventional bank lending, rather than funneling the bulk of loans to already-established business owners. If you fall into one of these groups, it's worth stating that clearly in your application rather than assuming it will be inferred from your documents.

## Loan Amounts, Markup, and Repayment Terms

This is the section where sources disagree the most, so read the range as a range, not a promise. Reported loan floors and ceilings vary from **Rs. 50,000** at the low end up to **Rs. 1 Million**, **Rs. 10 Million (1 Crore)**, or in some descriptions of the larger SME-focused tier, figures as high as **10 lakh rupees** for **collateral free loans** and up to **3 crore rupees** for SME financing under the Asaan Karobar Finance Scheme. Some pages split the loan into two categories — a **Clean Financing** tier requiring only a **personal guarantee** and no **property mortgage**, and a **Secured Financing** tier for larger amounts that does require mortgaged property.

On cost, the recurring theme is **zero markup** or **zero interest** on smaller loans, based on a **Qarz-e-Hasna** (interest-free lending) structure, with **subsidized interest rates** rather than full interest-free terms on larger tiers. Repayment structures mentioned include a **grace period** (commonly around **12 months**) before repayment starts, then **monthly installments** or **quarterly installments** over a total tenure that different sources describe as **3-5 year tenure** or **5 years repayment**. A processing or **administrative fee** is mentioned on some larger loans, with one source citing a specific figure — that figure is not consistent across sources, so we're not repeating it as fact here. **None of these numbers are confirmed against the official PSIC or Bank of Punjab portal in this review**, since both were unreachable at the time of writing (see the note at the end of this guide) — confirm your exact loan ceiling, markup, and fee structure on the current official application portal before you plan your business budget around any specific figure.

## Documents You'll Need

Gather these before you start the online form, so you're not scrambling mid-application:

- **CNIC copy** (front and back)
- **Passport-size photo**
- **Proof of residence** — typically a recent **utility bill**
- **Business plan** or **feasibility summary** describing what you intend to do with the loan
- **Bank account** details (**IBAN**)
- **Educational certificates** or **skill certificates**, where relevant to the business type
- **Domicile**, if requested for your specific case
- **Guarantor details**, for loan tiers that require a personal guarantee rather than collateral

Documents that are blurred, mismatched, or incomplete are named repeatedly as a common cause of application delay — scan clearly and double-check that the name and CNIC number on every document match exactly.

## How to Apply Online

The application process described across sources is fully digital, structured roughly as follows:

1. Visit the **official portal** for the loan category you're applying to (**karobar.punjab.gov.pk** is cited for the Asaan Karobar Finance route; the original Punjab Rozgar Program uses a separate PSIC-linked portal).
2. Create an account with your **CNIC number** and **mobile number**, completing **OTP verification** where required.
3. Fill in **personal details** and **business details**, including your loan purpose and business plan summary.
4. **Upload documents** from the list above, in clear, legible scans.
5. Submit the application — you'll typically receive an **application ID** for tracking.
6. Your submission moves into a **verification stage**, then an **approval stage**, before a bank representative makes contact about next steps.

Because there are at least two active portals depending on which loan tier and program name you're pursuing, and because the primary PSIC Rozgar portal was not reachable during this review, double-check you're on the current, correctly-spelled official domain before entering your CNIC anywhere — a wave of impersonation pages ride on this scheme's popularity (more on that below).

## Verification and Disbursement Timeline

After submission, expect **NADRA verification** of your CNIC, a **credit history check**, and a **business evaluation** of the plan you submitted, before **final bank approval**. Turnaround time is described in some sources as ranging from **24 hours to 15 days**, depending on **application completeness** and how much manual verification your file needs — an incomplete or inconsistent application is the most commonly cited reason for delay past that window.

Disbursement, once approved, typically comes through **Bank of Punjab** — either as a **Digital SME Card**, a **bank transfer**, or (for the Asaan Karobar Card tier) a **revolving credit system** intended for **working capital** and controlled **cash withdrawals** rather than a single lump sum.

## Eligible Business Types

The scheme is described as open to a wide range of sectors rather than one narrow category:

- **Agriculture**, **dairy**, and **livestock**
- **Retail shops** and **wholesale trading**
- **IT and freelancing**, including remote and **online businesses**
- **E-commerce** stores
- **Tailoring**, **beauty salons**, and other service-based trades
- **Manufacturing units** and small **workshops**
- **Food carts** and **restaurants**
- **Transport and logistics** services
- **Home-based work**, including **graphic design** and other freelance skills

Almost any legal, viable small business idea is described as eligible, provided the **business plan** you submit is specific and realistic rather than vague.

## Where to Get Help

If you get stuck mid-application, the channels named across sources include **Bank of Punjab branches**, **provincial industry and commerce offices**, an **official helpline**, and in some descriptions, **district-level facilitation centers**. Once you're registered, most portals also show an **application status** dashboard where you can track your file and upload any missing document the verification team requests.

A practical caution worth repeating here: **none of these schemes charge a fee to "guarantee" approval.** If anyone — including someone claiming to be a bank or PSIC representative — asks for payment to unlock or speed up your loan, that is not part of the legitimate process.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is the CM Punjab Rozgar Scheme the same as the Punjab Rozgar Scheme?**
They're closely related but not always the same program. "Punjab Rozgar Scheme" (or "Punjab Rozgar Program") is the original PSIC-run scheme dating to 2020. "CM Punjab Rozgar Scheme" is often used online as a search-friendly umbrella term that can refer to that same PSIC scheme, or to the newer Asaan Karobar Finance Scheme launched under the current Chief Minister. Confirm which specific portal and program a given source is describing before you rely on its figures.

**Is the loan really interest-free?**
Smaller loan tiers are commonly described as interest-free (zero markup), based on a Qarz-e-Hasna structure. Larger tiers are more often described as subsidized-markup rather than fully interest-free. Which applies to you depends on the loan category and amount — confirm the exact terms for your tier before applying.

**Do I need collateral or a personal guarantee?**
For smaller "Clean Financing" style loans, sources describe a personal guarantee as sufficient, with no property required. For larger, "Secured Financing" style loans, a property mortgage is described as a requirement. This is another detail that varies by loan tier, so check which category your application falls under.

**Can women apply?**
Yes — women entrepreneurs are named as a priority group across every source reviewed, alongside youth, persons with disabilities, and transgender individuals.

**What is the age limit?**
Most sources cite an age band of roughly 18 to 50 years, with one source noting professionals may qualify up to 57. Treat this as an approximate range and confirm the specific limit for your loan category on application.

**How much loan amount can I actually get?**
This is the single most inconsistent figure across sources, ranging from as low as Rs. 50,000 up to figures reported as high as Rs. 1 crore or, for a separate larger SME tier, up to Rs. 3 crore. Don't budget a business plan around any one number from a third-party site — confirm your actual eligible amount once your application and business category are reviewed.

**What documents do I need to prepare in advance?**
At minimum: your CNIC (both sides), a passport-size photo, proof of residence, a business plan, and your bank account/IBAN details. Some applicants will also need educational or skill certificates and guarantor details, depending on the loan tier.

**How long does approval take?**
Sources describe a range of 24 hours to 15 days after submission, depending on how complete your application is and how much manual verification is required.

**What happens if I miss a repayment installment?**
None of the sources reviewed give a detailed penalty structure, but all agree that missed payments affect your credit history and can affect eligibility for future government loan schemes. If you're at risk of missing a payment, contacting the issuing bank before the due date is the consistent recommendation.

**Which businesses qualify?**
A broad range — agriculture, retail, IT and freelancing, e-commerce, manufacturing, food service, transport, and home-based work are all named as eligible categories, provided the business plan submitted is specific and realistic.

**Is there a fee to apply?**
A processing or administrative fee is mentioned for some larger loan tiers, though the exact amount is not consistent across sources and isn't repeated here as fact. What is consistent: no legitimate version of this scheme requires payment to "guarantee" or speed up approval — treat any such request as a scam attempt.
