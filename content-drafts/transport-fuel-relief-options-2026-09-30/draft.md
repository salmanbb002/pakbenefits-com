## What Are Pakistan's Transport & Fuel Relief Options in 2026?

Pakistan's transport and fuel relief measures are government programs that lower the cost of getting around — either by cutting the price of petrol at the pump, paying a monthly cash subsidy directly to riders, or making public transport free. They were triggered by two sharp fuel-price shocks in 2026, so the options now on the table are layered rather than a single national scheme.

The first wave came in early April 2026, when a fuel-price hike pushed petrol past Rs 458 per litre and forced the provinces to respond. Punjab and Islamabad made public transport free, Sindh announced a Rs 2,000 monthly subsidy for motorcyclists, and KPK matched it for bikers and rickshaw owners. The second, larger wave came in mid-September 2026, when the Gulf crisis tied to the US–Iran conflict drove prices up again and Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif announced a federal Rs 100-per-litre relief scheme. Understanding which wave a scheme belongs to matters, because the amounts, dates and even the registration channels differ.

---

## The PM Fuel Relief Scheme: Rs 100 Per Litre for Bikes, Rickshaws and 800cc Cars

### What the Federal Scheme Gives You

The PM Fuel Relief Scheme, announced by Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif on 13 September 2026, cuts **Rs 100 off every litre of petrol** for three categories of vehicles: motorcycles, rickshaws, and small cars up to 800cc. The discount is applied at the pump, so a rider pays the subsidised rate at the point of sale rather than claiming a refund later.

The quota is capped per vehicle per month — **20 litres for bikes** and **30 litres for 800cc cars** — so the relief is targeted at low-income commuters rather than open-ended fuel consumption. The scheme was later widened to include **20-year-old motorcycles and rickshaws** after initial registration rules excluded older vehicles. Over **5 million people** registered in the first days, and the government began reimbursing **8,500 petrol stations** for the discount they give at the forecourt.

### Who Qualifies and What It Costs the Government

To qualify you must own a registered bike, rickshaw or 800cc car and register through the official channel — SMS to the short code **9771** or the designated portal. Registration is free; the government scrapped the earlier SMS charge after public pushback. The federal scheme is budgeted at roughly **Rs 75 billion**, with analysts estimating it costs up to **Rs 30 billion a month** if uptake is broad, and the first tranche of **Rs 1.2 billion** was disbursed digitally to about **32,000 beneficiaries**.

One caveat to track: Finance Minister Ishaq Dar has signalled that the fuel subsidy is a **temporary measure** and could be discontinued with the Prime Minister's approval once prices stabilise. As of late September 2026 the scheme is live, but treat the Rs 100/litre rate as volatile — verify it before relying on it.

## Punjab's Relief Package: Free Transport, Rs 100/Litre and a Rs 100,000 EV Reward

### The Rs 124 Billion "Sasta Petrol" Package

Punjab, under Chief Minister Maryam Nawaz Sharif, runs the broadest provincial package. The centrepiece is a **Rs 100-per-litre petrol subsidy for registered motorbike owners**, delivered alongside free public transport. The combined package was announced under a **Rs 124 billion "Sasta Petrol" (cheap petrol) relief program** in April 2026 and expanded as prices rose again.

Punjab's biker subsidy works on the same per-litre logic as the federal scheme but is administered provincially, so the two are not the same thing even though both quote "Rs 100 per litre." Punjab also removed **motorcycle registration and transfer fees** and sought **Rs 24 billion** in funding to sustain the biker subsidy as the relief measures grew.

### The Rs 100,000 Petrol-to-Electric Conversion Reward

Punjab's most distinctive offer is a **Rs 100,000 reward for converting a petrol bike to electric**. Instead of subsidising fuel forever, this grant pays bike owners to remove their petrol consumption entirely — a structural alternative the federal per-litre scheme does not offer. The program sits alongside the separate CM Punjab Electric Bike Scheme, which gives students a **Rs 90,000 subsidy** toward a PKR 199,000 electric scooty with zero down payment and ~Rs 3,000 monthly installments.

## Sindh's Rs 2,000 Monthly Fuel Subsidy for Motorcyclists

Sindh took a different approach: a flat **Rs 2,000 monthly fuel subsidy for motorcyclists**, announced in April 2026 as part of a **Rs 35 billion relief package**. Instead of discounting each litre at the pump, the province pays a fixed cash amount each month to registered riders, which cushions the fuel-price rise without the per-litre accounting the federal scheme needs.

Sindh also **reduced tax on ride-hailing services** to lower transport costs and announced a targeted **public-transport fare subsidy** to keep bus and van fares stable. Registration runs through a dedicated **Sindh online portal**, and the scheme is aimed at the province's large motorcycle-commuter base. The monthly, flat-cash design means your benefit is fixed — it does not rise or fall with how much fuel you actually buy.

---

## KPK's Rs 2,000 Fuel Subsidy for Bikers and Rickshaw Owners

Khyber Pakhtunkhwa matched Sindh's number but widened the audience: a **Rs 2,000 monthly fuel subsidy for bikers and rickshaw owners**, announced in early April 2026. The KPK government also launched a broader **fuel support program for motorbike and scooter owners**, aiming to cover more than **1 million bikers**.

As with Sindh, the KPK subsidy is a fixed monthly cash relief rather than a pump discount. The province has run into the same verification questions that followed Sindh's rollout — some early claims about registration and disbursement were corrected — so the practical guidance is the same: register only through the **official KPK channel** and treat third-party "agents" as a red flag. KPK has separately run a fuel subsidy for commercial vehicles, which is a distinct scheme from the biker program.

## Free Public Transport in Punjab and Islamabad

Free public transport is the simplest relief option because it needs no registration. In April 2026, **Punjab made its Metro and Orange Line public transport free** across cities, and **Islamabad followed** by making its Metro and bus services free for the public. The move was explicitly framed as a response to the "abrupt" fuel-price hike, giving commuters a way to avoid buying fuel altogether.

The trade-off is scope: free transit only helps people who live on a serviced route. A motorcyclist in a town with no Metro line gets no benefit from it, which is why Punjab paired free transport with the per-litre biker subsidy. Islamabad's free-transport announcement covered a defined period (reported as one month initially), so check whether the waiver is still active on the relevant authority's page before planning around it.

## Electric Bike Schemes: The Fuel-Free Alternative

### CM Punjab E-Bike Scheme and PM PAVE

The electric bike schemes are the structural answer to fuel relief: instead of discounting petrol, they subsidise the vehicle that does not need it. The **CM Punjab Electric Bike Scheme (Phase 2)** provides **100,000 electric scooties** to students at **PKR 199,000**, with a **Rs 90,000 government subsidy**, 0% interest, no down payment and ~**Rs 3,000 monthly installments** over three years. Applications run through **bikes.punjab.gov.pk**, and the Phase 2 window closes **October 4, 2026**.

The federal **Pakistan Accelerated Vehicle Electrification (PAVE)** program is the nationwide equivalent, offering **Rs 50,000 for bank-leased** two-wheelers or up to **Rs 80,000 for self-financed** ones through **pave.gov.pk**. Balochistan has launched its own subsidised electric bike scheme for students and women. For a daily commuter, an e-bike removes the recurring fuel bill that every per-litre or monthly subsidy only partly offsets — which is why analysts increasingly point to electrification as the durable fix.

## Which Relief Option Is Right for You?

Choosing comes down to three questions: what you drive, where you live, and whether you want short-term relief or a permanent fix.

- **If you ride a motorbike and live in Punjab** — register for the Punjab Rs 100/litre subsidy, and seriously weigh the Rs 100,000 conversion reward or the student e-bike scheme if you qualify; both remove the petrol bill rather than discount it.
- **If you ride a motorbike in Sindh or KPK** — the Rs 2,000 monthly subsidy is yours, but remember it is a fixed amount, not a per-litre saving.
- **If you drive a rickshaw or an 800cc car** — the federal PM Fuel Relief Scheme is your main option, with a 20–30 litre monthly cap.
- **If you use public transport in Punjab or Islamabad** — the free-transit waiver needs no registration, but confirm the waiver window is still open.
- **If you want to stop paying for fuel entirely** — the electric bike schemes (CM Punjab, PAVE, Balochistan) are the only options that eliminate the recurring cost rather than cushion it.

A word of caution on stacking: the federal and provincial schemes are administered by different departments, and being approved for one does not automatically register you for another. Apply to each you qualify for through its own official channel, and never through an agent.

## How to Apply for Each Relief Option

Each scheme has its own channel — there is no single national registration form. Follow the official route for the one you qualify for:

1. **PM Fuel Relief Scheme** — send your CNIC and vehicle details to short code **9771**, or register on the Petroleum Division's designated portal. Registration is free; the earlier SMS charge was scrapped.
2. **Punjab biker subsidy** — register your motorbike on the official Punjab portal (announced alongside the "Sasta Petrol" package).
3. **Punjab EV conversion reward** — apply through the Punjab government portal; your petrol bike must be converted to electric to claim the Rs 100,000.
4. **Sindh fuel subsidy** — register on the Sindh government's online portal using your CNIC and motorcycle registration.
5. **KPK fuel subsidy** — register through the official KPK channel for bikers and rickshaw owners.
6. **CM Punjab E-Bike Scheme** — apply at **bikes.punjab.gov.pk** before **October 4, 2026** (students only).
7. **PM PAVE** — apply at **pave.gov.pk**.

Before you begin, keep your **CNIC** and your vehicle's **registration number** ready, and make sure the mobile number you use is registered in your own name — this is the single most common reason registration fails.

## Official Portals, Helplines and Scam Alerts

Relief schemes attract fraud. Keep these rules in mind:

- Registration for every scheme above is **free** — never pay an agent or "middleman" who promises faster approval.
- Only trust **.gov.pk** domains and the official short code **9771** for the federal scheme. Domains that look like "petrolrelief.com" or "subsidy-online.net" are not government sites.
- Never share your **CNIC image, OTP, bank PIN or wallet PIN** with anyone.
- Never send money through JazzCash, Easypaisa or any wallet to "unlock" your subsidy.
- Per-litre discounts are applied **at the pump** — you do not pay first and get reimbursed by a stranger.
- For complaints about petrol pumps refusing the Rs 100/litre discount, use the government's official complaint channel rather than a private fixer.

Confirm the current status, amounts and registration channels on the relevant official portal before relying on any figure in this guide.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is Pakistan's transport and fuel relief for 2026?

Pakistan's 2026 relief combines a federal Rs 100-per-litre petrol discount for bikes, rickshaws and 800cc cars, monthly cash subsidies in Sindh and KPK (Rs 2,000), free public transport in Punjab and Islamabad, and electric bike schemes such as the CM Punjab E-Bike Scheme and PM PAVE.

### How much petrol discount does the PM Fuel Relief Scheme give?

The PM Fuel Relief Scheme gives Rs 100 off every litre of petrol, capped at 20 litres per month for bikes and 30 litres per month for 800cc cars. It was announced on 13 September 2026.

### Do I have to pay to register for the fuel relief scheme?

No. Registration is free. The government scrapped the earlier SMS charge, and the official channel is SMS to short code 9771 or the designated portal.

### Is the Rs 100/litre fuel subsidy permanent?

No. Finance Minister Ishaq Dar has signalled the fuel subsidy is temporary and could be discontinued once prices stabilise. As of late September 2026 it is live, but the rate should be treated as volatile.

### What is the difference between Punjab's and the federal Rs 100/litre subsidy?

Both discount petrol by Rs 100 per litre, but the federal scheme is run by the Petroleum Division for bikes, rickshaws and 800cc cars nationwide, while Punjab's subsidy is a provincial program for registered motorbike owners. They are separate registrations.

### How do Sindh and KPK's fuel subsidies differ from Punjab's?

Sindh and KPK pay a fixed Rs 2,000 monthly cash subsidy to motorcyclists (KPK also covers rickshaw owners), whereas Punjab discounts petrol by Rs 100 per litre at the pump. The monthly amount is fixed and does not change with how much fuel you buy.

### Who qualifies for the CM Punjab Electric Bike Scheme?

Students aged 16 or above enrolled in a recognised public or private institution qualify, subject to a guarantor with a minimum monthly income of PKR 40,000. The e-bike is priced at PKR 199,000 with a Rs 90,000 subsidy and ~Rs 3,000 monthly installments.

### What is the Rs 100,000 petrol-to-electric conversion reward?

Punjab offers a Rs 100,000 grant to petrol-bike owners who convert their motorcycle to electric. It is a one-time reward designed to remove petrol consumption rather than subsidise it.

### Is free public transport still available?

Free public transport was announced in Punjab and Islamabad in April 2026 in response to a fuel-price hike. The Islamabad waiver was reported for an initial one-month period, so confirm whether it is still active with the relevant authority before relying on it.

### Can I apply for more than one relief scheme at the same time?

Being approved for one scheme does not automatically register you for another. If you qualify for multiple options — for example, a Punjab biker subsidy and the federal fuel scheme — apply to each through its own official channel.

### How do I avoid fuel-relief scams?

Register only through official .gov.pk portals and the short code 9771. Registration is free, discounts are applied at the pump, and you should never pay an agent, share an OTP, or send money to any wallet to claim relief.
