# Public Sector Development Programme (PSDP) 2026–27: Comprehensive Budget Allocation & Project Breakdown

The **Public Sector Development Programme (PSDP)** 2026–27 represents Pakistan’s **Federal PSDP** budget framework within a total **National Development Outlay** of **Rs 3,675 billion**. It allocates **Rs 1,000 billion** for **Federal PSDP**, **Rs 2,224 billion** for **Provincial ADPs**, and **Rs 451 billion** for **State-Owned Enterprises**, prioritizing **Water Resources Division**, **National Highway Authority**, and ongoing projects.

---

## What Is the Total Outlay and Structure of PSDP 2026–27?

The **National Economic Council** approved a total **National Development Outlay** of **Rs 3,675 billion** for Fiscal Year 2026–27 to drive national infrastructure, economic stabilization, and regional development across Pakistan.

The total development framework divides funding into three distinct operational channels:

| Budget Development Component | Approved Outlay (FY 2026–27) | Share of Total Outlay (%) | Primary Operational Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Federal PSDP** | **Rs 1,000 billion** | 27.2% | Federal ministries, strategic infrastructure & **CPEC 2.0 Initiatives** |
| **Provincial ADPs** | **Rs 2,224 billion** | 60.5% | Provincial public works, healthcare, education & local roads |
| **State-Owned Enterprises** | **Rs 451 billion** | 12.3% | Off-budget self-financed corporate utility projects |
| **Total National Outlay** | **Rs 3,675 billion** | **100.0%** | Comprehensive national capital investment program |

Within the **Rs 1,000 billion** **Federal PSDP** allocation, Rs 682.485 billion funds federal ministries and divisions, while Rs 312.515 billion supports major state-owned corporate entities like the **National Highway Authority** and power utilities. An additional Rs 4 billion covers project liabilities, and Rs 1 billion initiates special **CPEC 2.0 Initiatives**.

To inspect official document releases, citizens can reference the published schedules on the **Ministry of Planning, Development and Special Initiatives** portal.

## How Does PSDP 2026–27 Align with the 5Es Strategy and URAAN Pakistan?

The **Public Sector Development Programme** framework directly operationalizes the government’s **URAAN Pakistan** economic transformation blueprint and the **13th Five Year Plan** through the targeted **5Es Strategy**.

The **5Es Strategy** framework establishes five strategic pillars designed to solve structural economic bottlenecks while maintaining strict fiscal discipline:

1. **Exports:** Accelerating industrial productivity, export processing zones, and technological competitiveness.
2. **E-Pakistan:** Digitizing public services, expanding IT infrastructure, and enhancing youth tech skills.
3. **Environment:** Funding climate-resilient water storage, flood mitigation, and clean energy transitions.
4. **Energy & Infrastructure:** Building power transmission lines and expanding multi-modal transport highways.
5. **Equity & Empowerment:** Investing in human capital development, special geographic regions (AJK and Gilgit-Baltistan), and targeted social protection.

By aligning every project approval with these five pillars, the **Ministry of Planning, Development and Special Initiatives** ensures public capital spending creates measurable economic returns rather than unviable fiscal assets.

## Which Sectors and Ministries Received the Highest PSDP Allocations?

The transport infrastructure and water management sectors secured the largest funding allocations under the **Federal PSDP** 2026–27 to address critical energy and logistics constraints.

The corporate and ministerial allocations reflect key national priorities:

- **National Highway Authority:** Received the single largest corporate allocation of Rs 224.5 billion to complete motorways, national highways, and trade corridors.
- **Water Resources Division:** Earmarked Rs 103 billion overall, with Rs 74.92 billion specifically dedicated to 35 core water conservation, dam construction, and flood protection projects.
- **Gwadar Development Projects:** Allocated over Rs 14.2 billion for the **Gwadar Port / M-8 Corridor**, New Gwadar International Airport operationalization, and municipal water infrastructure.
- **Karakoram Highway Relocation:** Designated Rs 5 billion for the 102-km Thakot–Raikot section relocation to safeguard vital international trade arteries.
- **Cabinet Division & Special Areas:** Granted Rs 30 billion for local community schemes alongside Rs 22.15 billion for Azad Jammu & Kashmir (AJK) and Gilgit-Baltistan (GB).

These allocations ensure that strategic physical assets receive sustained funding, preserving trade logistics across northern and southern corridors.

## Why Did the Government Prioritize Ongoing Schemes Over New Projects?

The federal government imposed strict caps on new development schemes during FY 2026–27 to prevent the accumulation of unsustainable **Throw-forward Liabilities**.

Faced with ministerial demands exceeding Rs 4.1 trillion against a capped **Rs 1,000 billion** federal budget, the **Ministry of Planning, Development and Special Initiatives** established clear entry criteria for new proposals:

- **Ongoing Project Priority:** More than 85% of total **PSDP** funds are reserved for near-completion projects to ensure fast completion.
- **Restriction on Unapproved Schemes:** Unapproved local projects without secured funding lines were excluded from the main development portfolio.
- **Exceptions for Critical Needs:** New project inclusions were strictly restricted to national security initiatives, emergency disaster recovery, and foreign-funded commitments.

This disciplined approach reduces project gestation periods and prevents cost overruns caused by delayed fund dispersion across thousands of underfunded schemes.

## What Is the First Quarter (Q1) Fund Release and Utilization Status?

During the first quarter (July–September 2026) of FY 2026–27, the federal government executed its **Quarterly Release Mechanism**, authorizing Rs 220.72 billion in development funds, representing approximately 22% of the annual **Federal PSDP** allocation.

The initial performance audit reveals steady fund releases alongside initial execution phases:

- **Total Funds Authorized:** Rs 220.721 billion authorized by the **Planning Commission** (Rs 187.5 billion for ministries, Rs 33.216 billion for corporations).
- **Actual Q1 Utilization:** Total actual expenditure reached Rs 61.320 billion (6.13% of the total annual budget).
- **Top Authorized Ministry:** The **Water Resources Division** received the largest Q1 authorization of Rs 86.517 billion to maintain summer construction momentum on major dams.

The low early utilization rate reflects initial procurement and verification cycles standard in public sector execution, with spending expected to accelerate sharply in Q2 and Q3.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### What is the total federal PSDP allocation for FY 2026–27?
The total **Federal PSDP** allocation for Fiscal Year 2026–27 is **Rs 1,000 billion**. This forms part of the broader **Rs 3,675 billion** **National Development Outlay** approved by the **National Economic Council**.

### Who approves the Public Sector Development Programme in Pakistan?
The **National Economic Council**, chaired by the Prime Minister of Pakistan and comprising provincial Chief Ministers and federal ministers, formally approves the **PSDP**.

### Which sector received the highest corporate funding in PSDP 2026–27?
The **National Highway Authority** received the highest corporate allocation of Rs 224.5 billion under the corporate development budget block for road and motorway networks.

### What are the 5Es included in the PSDP 2026–27 strategic framework?
The **5Es Strategy** framework consists of Exports, E-Pakistan, Environment, Energy & Infrastructure, and Equity & Empowerment.

### How much money was authorized during Q1 (July–September 2026) of PSDP 2026–27?
The **Planning Commission** authorized Rs 220.721 billion (22% of the federal allocation) under the **Quarterly Release Mechanism** during the first quarter of FY 2026–27.

### Why are new development projects limited in the PSDP 2026–27 budget?
New projects are restricted to prevent cost overruns and curb **Throw-forward Liabilities**, ensuring ongoing high-priority projects receive adequate funding to reach completion.

### How much budget is allocated for provincial development programs (ADPs) in 2026–27?
**Provincial ADPs** have been allocated a total outlay of **Rs 2,224 billion** across all four provinces for local public development.

### What is the role of the Planning Commission in PSDP fund releases?
The **Planning Commission**, under the **Ministry of Planning, Development and Special Initiatives**, authorizes quarterly releases, monitors project execution, and conducts performance audits on public spending.

### Are foreign-funded development projects included in PSDP 2026–27?
Yes, foreign-funded development projects are prioritized and granted exceptions under the new project entry criteria to ensure international commitment compliance.

### Where can citizens download the official PSDP 2026–27 budget document?
Citizens can view and download the complete **PSDP** 2026–27 allocation document on the official website of the **Ministry of Planning, Development and Special Initiatives** (`pc.gov.pk`).
